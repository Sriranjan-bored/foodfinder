🛒 SNAP (food stamps)

What it is: A monthly benefit on an EBT card that buys groceries. It does not replace food pantries — most families use both.

Who qualifies: Households with limited income and savings. The income limit is roughly 130% of the federal poverty line, and there are deductions for rent, childcare, and medical costs — so it's worth applying even if you think you won't qualify.

How to apply: Call 2-1-1, apply online through your state's benefits portal, or walk into any SNAP office in the directory above.

What to bring: Photo ID, proof of address, pay stubs or a benefit letter, and Social Security numbers for people in your household. You can start an application without every document.

🤰 WIC

What it is: Food, formula, and breastfeeding support for pregnant and postpartum parents, infants, and children under 5.

Who qualifies: Income-based, plus a nutrition or health risk assessment done at the appointment. Working families often qualify.

How to apply: Call your local WIC office or 2-1-1 to schedule. Bring ID, proof of address, proof of income, and your child if they're being enrolled.

🎒 Free & reduced school meals

Every public school district accepts free/reduced meal applications. Many schools are now "community eligible," which means all students eat free with no application at all.

During summer and school breaks, the Summer Food Service Program serves free meals to anyone 18 and under — no registration, no ID. Look for "School meals" listings in the directory.

Why I built this

Last year I watched a neighbor spend an entire afternoon calling around to find out which pantry was open — and then get turned away because she didn't know she needed to bring an ID. The information existed. It was just scattered across flyers, Facebook posts, and five different organizations' websites.

FoodFinder puts it in one place: every free food resource in the area on a single map, with hours, eligibility, and what-to-bring details shown before you make the trip.

The directory is built to be usable by people who are stressed, short on time, on a slow phone, or reading in their second language. No login. No forms. No data collected.

Where this goes next

  • Crowd-sourced updates. "The pantry is out of produce today" — reported by neighbors, in real time.
  • SMS access. Text a ZIP code, get the three nearest open pantries. People without a smartphone or data plan are exactly the people who need this most.
  • Live data. Sync with 211 and local open-data feeds so listings update automatically instead of going stale.
  • Multi-language and low-literacy modes. Icon-first navigation and a read-aloud option.
  • Seasonal highlighting. Summer meal sites in June, holiday food drives in November.

Built with

  • HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript
  • Leaflet + OpenStreetMap (no API key, no cost)
  • A single flat JSON data file — no backend
  • localStorage for saved resources and community submissions

Accessibility

  • Keyboard-navigable map, list, and modal
  • Full list view for anyone who finds maps hard to use
  • Color + icon + text on every status badge
  • Large tap targets on mobile
